**Chapter 1**

Saber’s hooves arrhythmically pounded the ground as Estin rode towards Carlton. Estin rubbed the mane of the horse Saber was currently inhabiting, encouraging him to push just a little further. They were nearing the border of town, and Saber could rest soon, once again disembodied. Then Estin’s real work would begin.

Estin was staring off into the distance, nearly asleep, then shook himself awake when he saw lights in the distance. He tugged sharply on Saber’s reins, causing him to rear up, and exerted control over the horse, stopping the neigh that was already forming in his mouth. Estin dismounted and put his right hand on Saber’s sweaty face, then focused. In his left hand Saber’s intelligence formed, and the horse collapsed to the ground, mindless. Estin carefully put the horse’s intelligence in his small bag, storing it below his many other intelligences.

“Slivers!” Estin softly cursed, looking at the dead horse laying near the road. Someone may see that. But … I gotta go. No time for more mistakes. I need to get back to Emily!

He ran down the road as fast as he could until he began to hear voices, then slowed. He stepped off the road, on the opposite side as the lights, a few paces into the woods, making him next-to invisible to travelers. He hurried forwards, cringing each time he made a noise, but couldn’t let himself slow down.

He slowly approached the huge clearing in the woods that Carlton was nestled in, and cursed again when he saw what the lights were.

There were people digging behind what looked like their town’s chapel, led by the light of lanterns hung from posts in the ground. Teams of three or four were working in one space, and Estin counted about a few less than a dozen of these teams at work.

They were warned, Estin realized. But by who?

As he watched, he saw one of the men in the nearby team turn away from the hole he was digging suddenly. Estin couldn’t quite hear, but he thought the man was retching. He heard the group call out, and saw someone run over to them. The runner handed off what appeared to be a hammer, and Estin saw the person in the hole swing it again and again into the grave. After a few seconds, the man handed the hammer to someone else and climbed out of the hole, holding a rag over his face.

They found out about the bones, Estin realized, clenching his fists and crouching down further. He dug his palms into his eyes and sat for a second, clenching his teeth and seething. If I could just get ahead, just be left alone for a few days … could I go to Fardrax? But no, that’s too far East … I need these bodies. And I need to get home.

He focused, closing his eyes, and relaxing. He quested out with his mind, and felt Kaleyla flying high above. He got a faint sense of her feathered form flapping to gain height, and sighed. She wasn’t far away, but he knew from experience that he couldn’t establish a connection at this distance without getting a piercing headache.

Kay, I need eyes on the graveyard, Estin sent, and frowned as he sensed a flash of panic sent back his way. Please.

They might see me, Kay sent back a few seconds later, her mental voice hesitant. I might give you away.

No, you won’t, Estin sent, but he sensed Kay flapping vigorously to gain height. He shook his head, but wasn’t surprised. Stay safe, he sent, trying to keep frustration out of his tone.

He watched for a few more minutes, eying the people, and then heard a banging at a nearby door. He crouched down out of reflex, listening intently.

“Steph, you lazybones! Get out of your sliving house! The General ordered every man to work on this!”

Estin saw a man carrying a lantern pass between buildings and heard him bang on the next door and say roughly the same thing. Estin waited a few more minutes, and then sprinted from the woods between two of the houses on the outskirts of the village. He hopped a wooden fence, and walked out from between the houses at the same time that a man, presumably Steph, stumbled out of his door. Estin slowed his gate, trying to not look at the man and acting as if he had just been woken up too.

“Hey you!” The man yelled, and Estin turned his face slightly towards him, not wanting to let the man see his full face.

This is the worst idea I’ve ever had, Estin realized. I could’ve just run, I could’ve left, I didn’t have to run in here, I-

“They woke you up too?” Steph said, his words slightly slurred.

“Y-yeah,” Estin stuttered as Steph walked up to him and put his arm around him. Estin wrinkled his nose and saw he was carrying a small bottle.

“Absurd, it is,” Steph muttered as he stumbled towards the graveyard, Estin tense and wishing he could just run. “A man, raising te’ dead? So we needa dig up our ancestors and crack their bones into hundreds of pieces? Into slivers I guess, right? Haha!”

“Yeah, real bizarre,” Estin said, faking a laugh, and Steph joined in, then belched in Estin’s face. Estin grimaced, holding his breath. They continued stumbling towards the graveyard, Estin holding Steph more than being led by him.

“Some hero that Kadahn is, eh? Not a hero if I have to wake up for him, though, am I right?”

He laughed again, but Estin’s frown deepened. So it’s General Kadahn’s orders. But how do they know? We’re half a day’s ride from the capital … I can’t believe this is my reward for helping him.

A few more dark figures shambled past towards the graveyard and Estin heard more knocks on doors behind them; apparently Steph wasn’t the only person who tried to skip out on this unseemly work.

They approached the graveyard, Estin still held tight by the drunk man for support, and Estin tightened his fists and clenched his teeth. This was the dangerous moment. Were they given descriptions of my likeness? Told to interrogate travelers? If they know about the bones, they may know more.

They neared the fence, and Estin snuck his right hand into his pocket, fingering his scalpel. He tightened his grip on Steph’s neck slightly, and visualized the spot he’d target if he needed to. If anyone looked remotely suspicious of him, he would—No. No, I swore to myself I would never break that rule.

A woman was standing at the gate, near the short iron fence. Estin tensed as the woman turned the lantern towards them, and kept his head ducked down. “Who are-” he heard the woman start saying, then Steph beside him roared.

“I’m Steph, ya know me! And this here’s me friend, helped me ‘tay upright on the walk over here. Give me one of yer best shovels, so the mayor back there don’t holler after me no more.”

Estin didn’t look up, too afraid that he might be called out, but the light illuminating his feet faded and he took a shaky breath. The woman came back with two shovels, and Estin took his without making eye contact. Steph walked painfully slowly, towards the nearest group of men, who were about knees deep digging in a grave, still towing Estin. Steph then started leaning to the side, and Estin managed to extricate himself as Steph collapsed to the ground, dropping his shovel in the process.

“Drunk buffoon,” Estin heard the taller of the two men say, and he awkwardly joined in with their laughs as Steph stood back up.

Steph tried to rise, using the handle of the shovel as a cane, but fell backwards. The shorter man, his face barely lit by the lantern at the grave, sighed, and then set down his shovel.

“I’ll take him home. Best, honestly, he’s too drunk to tell a corpse apart from one of us.”

Estin gave him room as the shorter man slowly lifted Steph and got him lumbering back towards the gate.

“Worthless drunk,” the taller man said, still digging. He waved to Estin and he quickly approached the grave and started digging, too. “Slivers, hopefully that man goes in one of these holes soon,” he added, and Estin frowned.

They dug in silence for a minute, Estin’s shoulders tense and his palms sweating as he glanced around every few moments, looking for signs that anyone was looking his way. He reached out to Kay a few times, but all he felt from her was a deep sense of unease. But for the most part, people seemed to be keeping their heads down and digging.

Estin dug as quickly as he could, and felt himself quickly tiring. He tried to keep pace with the taller man, but he was already exhausted from sleep deprivation and riding most of the day. I need rest soon, Estin realized as his hands slipped on the shovel, a few splinters driving into his palms. He focused on them for a moment, exerting power until the splinters pushed out of his palm. As the small cuts in his hand began healing over, Estin turned more of his focus to digging.

“Come on, come on, hurry up,” the taller man said, glaring at Estin. “Slivers, you’re slow.”

Estin tightened his fingers on his shovel and dug it into the ground, digging up a large clod of dirt. Prick. I think I’ll call you that. Yeah, that’s right.

“What, you tongueless, too?” The taller man asked, turning to look at him closer.

“No,” Estin said quietly. Prick fits you well.

Prick glowered at him and adjusted his thick vest, then turned back to shoveling. Estin followed his lead, and they seemed to be digging a smaller hole, only a few feet wide. They only need to shatter a few bones to make these bodies worthless to me, Estin realized. The skull, or the spine.

As the hole narrowed, the taller man waved Estin off and he stepped out of the hole. It was nearing waist-depth and Estin took to shoveling the dirt around the hole out of the way so it wouldn’t slide back in.

“Slivers to all this, and slivers to you!” Prick said, cursing at Estin as some dirt slid back into the hole. “How deep does this go?”

Estin clenched his fists, and looked around. No one was watching, and they were on the edge of the graveyard, the edge of the light. He grimaced. I can’t do it. I just can’t. He was about to turn around and run for the woods, when he thought of Emily. I need to protect her. This is the only way. But no … I swore to her that I’d keep my rules. But … technically I agreed to never use my power to harm.

“Well, are you going to help, or not?” Prick asked. His next shovelful of dirt slid off the pile, most of it falling back into the hole. “At least move that dirt out of my way.”

He paused, looking at Keph, and fingered the scalpel in his pocket, mentally cursing himself. Rule number two: do no harm with the power. But is it really harmful if I’m going to bring him back?

He thought about Emily, and Nynah, and he squeezed the razor tightly. I will NOT go back. They will NOT take me again. This will be … my one exception.

“Hey, there’s the body,” Estin said, bending down. Prick turned, squinting, as Estin hastily cast his gaze across the graveyard, verifying no one was nearby. They were on the edge of the digging group, a few graves removed from anyone else.

“Where? This doesn’t seem deep enough—”

“No, right there, bend down and look closer,” Estin said, leaning forward as he pulled the scalpel out of his pocket.

“What are you talking—”

Prick’s words were cut off as Estin jumped onto his back, driving his face into the dirt. Estin quickly pressed Prick’s face into the ground, driving the blade deep into his carotid artery and then through his vocal cords. Estin quickly put his knee on Prick’s head, and used his hand to push his jaw upwards, stopping him from speaking and accelerating the bleeding. He closed his eyes, exerting the little control he had over a living body to slightly slacken Prick’s muscles, making it harder for him to fight back. Another cut and a few seconds later and Prick’s movements began to slow down. Estin held his hand to the man’s head, concentrating, and felt his consciousness slam into a mental barrier. Pulling spheres out of living beings was always difficult, but as Estin bore down on him mentally, he realized this was the first time he had done this to a living person. Prick pushed back, mentally lashing out until Estin felt a shift, then Prick fell still.

An intelligence sphere formed in Estin’s hand, and the name Keph popped into his mind. He hesitated only for a moment before adding the sphere to the collection in his bag, putting it in a pocket near the top. He sat there for a moment, frozen in place, looking down at Keph’s motionless body. No, no, he’s not dead, Estin reminded himself, squeezing the sphere for reassurance. Not really. I'll bring you back later, when I'm done with your body, Estin sent to him mentally. In response, he felt confusion and a distinct sense of loss coming from the sphere. That was wrong. Estin dropped his scalpel, shuddering at the wetness on his hands. His work as a chirurgeon made him no stranger to blood, but this was different. I hurt him. I hurt him! I caused this damage! I swore I would only heal—I swore that to myself! To Emily.

He wanted to scream, to heal Keph’s body and put his mind back into it, to run away from the village and never look back. A shuffling sound had him straightening up in an instant. Emily needs me. She needs this.

Estin looked around, peeking his head out of the grave, and saw someone approaching. Hurriedly he grabbed his fighting sphere out of his bag and willed it to copy. In his left hand a darker, smaller copy of the sphere formed, and he hurriedly pressed it to Prick’s head while putting his original sphere in his bag.

“Everything all right?” Estin heard a voice say, and tensed, looking upwards. He put his right hand on Prick—no, Keph’s—neck, his left on the wall, and willed his body to heal.

“Yeah,” Estin said, using his hood to block his face. A squat man holding a sledge hammer was standing above him, although the lantern light from the other side of the graveyard made it hard to see his features. “Keph just, uh, fell.” Please, let it be too dark to see the blood.

“Is he awake?” The man asked, concern obvious in his voice.

“Yes, yes ...” Estin said, draining a little dirt away from the side of the hole as he repaired Keph’s neck. “You’re fine, aren’t you?”

Keph jolted upright as his neck healed, and Estin exerted control, causing him to grab his shovel.

“Slivers, that hurt,” Estin had Keph say. “I’m fine though.”

“Oh, you need a hammer? Got a body?”

“Sure,” Keph said, and Estin focused, having him reach to grab the proffered handle. “Just a few more shovel fulls and we’ll be there,” Estin had Keph add.

The squat man watched for a few seconds as Estin carefully pulled himself out of the hole and let Keph get to work. Keph dug out a few small scoops of dirt, grunting in exertion, and the squat man wandered off as someone called out to him. Estin stooped down, trying to stay out of sight while looking like he was working in case anyone saw him. He willed Keph to keep digging and scanned around, surveying the graveyard.

Most of the digging was happening to his right, on the other side of the graveyard. Most of the graves on the left half had been unopened, while a lot on the right had people actively digging or shoveling dirt back in, their work of destroying the bones apparently complete. To his left, the gravestones and grave-signs appeared to be in roughly the same shape. Hopefully the bodies are new enough, Estin thought. If they’re more than four or five decades old, I won’t be able to do much of anything with them.

Estin looked down as he heard Keph strike something hard and willed the man to stop. Estin had him bend down and rub at the dirt with his hands, and he saw a rough wooden slab. Carefully now, Estin thought, and willed Keph to punch at the edge of the wooden box with his shovel. Estin watched closely as Keph found the corner of the coffin and started working the edge of the shovel under the top board.

Keph spent about a minute carefully opening the box, and then climbed out as Estin lowered himself into the hole. Estin pushed the board upwards, leaning it against the side of the small hole, and looked inside the box. Inside was a mostly-rotted corpse, its clothing mostly tattered. Estin held his breath—the smell still irked him despite the countless times he had done this—and put his right hand on the side of the hole, his left hand on the half-rotted skull. He breathed in and out slowly, then gritted his teeth.

Estin saw sunken flesh suddenly puff back out, returning to its dark hue. He shivered as the air around him grew colder, but kept pushing. He heard tendons popping as they reformed and reconnected, and had to look away as the withered eyes regrew and then were covered in eyelids once more.

The whole body, covered only in rags, jerked once, then fell still. Estin rubbed his hands together, trying to heat them up, and quickly peeked out of the grave. Seeing no one approaching, he knelt back down and reached into his pack with his left hand. He grabbed the sphere on top, made a copy in his right hand, and pushed it into the head of the recently re-animated body.

Deep blue eyes shot open and he extricated himself at Estin’s command from the dirt that had slid back into the hole. He was nearly naked, somewhat muscled, wearing only the tattered remains of his simple shirt and pants, and Estin bit his lip - I don’t have anything for them to wear, he realized. If people see them walking around like this …

He turned back to Keph and had him squat down behind the mound of dirt and remove his trousers. He gave these to the reanimated man—I’ll call him Number One; he’s too long dead for me to save even a scrap of his intelligence—and Number One put them on before climbing out of the hole. Estin hefted himself out of the hole, already fatiguing from the exertion, and commanded Keph to head further away from the lights, where people wouldn’t notice he was only wearing his underwear.

At Estin’s command, Keph took with him one of the shovels, and Number One followed behind him with the other one. Estin joined Number One and Keph at the hole they were digging at one of the graves at the far corner of the graveyard. There were no lights nearby, and the two reanimated men were working feverishly, throwing shovel-fulls of dirt over their shoulders faster than it seemed possible that they could. Estin bent down next to them, commanded them to stop, and spent a moment draining some dirt from the hole they were digging to fix the muscle tears that they were already developing from their frantic work. They were digging faster than any normal humans could, but he’d have to keep repairing their bodies every few minutes at this rate. He finished quickly and sent them back to work.

He crouched down near the fence, letting the two men do the digging as fast as they could, repairing their muscles from time to time and trying to get as warm as he could. He drew his cloak around himself and tucked in his legs, doing his best to preserve body heat; he would be needing all the warmth he could get tonight.

In just a few minutes Number One and Keph were waist deep in the dirt. Estin admired his fighting intelligence’s precision as the men plowed on, digging more and more rapidly and nearly hitting each other with each shovel stroke, but always just barely missing.

He wrapped his arms around his legs, finally feeling somewhat warm again, although he knew in a few minutes he’d have another reanimation to do. I need a fire, something, anything to help me stay warm tonight, Estin thought. No way I can reanimate more than a dozen people without extra heat.

Estin perked up suddenly as he heard someone approaching. He heard the whining voice from earlier, the voice of the man who had been knocking on villager’s doors, and quickly stood, trying to act like he was helping his two reanimated. He slowed their efforts, trying to make them seem more natural and tired.

“I appreciate your efforts -” Estin heard the man say as he approached, “But please give the sledgehammer to someone who will use it. Don’t just leave it sitting there!”

Estin nodded quickly and stepped in between the approaching man and Number One, trying to hide their half-dressed shirtless bodies.

“And hurry up! The general said he wants this done before sunrise!”

“Yes sir!” Estin said, and he had Keph repeat him.

The bossy man nodded curtly, and then turned to walk back towards the main group.

Estin huddled again for a few minutes, warming himself further, until the frantic shoveling of his workers stopped. He shambled around the pile of dirt, crouching down to avoid being seen by anyone, and approached the hole, which the reanimated men were crawling out of. Estin looked inside and saw a small wooden coffin. He grimaced when he noticed the size, but lowered himself into the hole. He put his hand on the lid of the coffin, his other on the nearly-clean skull within, and concentrated.

He gritted his teeth and the air around him grew deathly chill. The coffin lid shrunk as the edges were eaten away at, and Estin then grabbed the edge of the coffin base, consuming that, too. The skull covered in flesh, the bones clicking back together as muscles, sinews and skin reformed, and then, one of the last steps, the eyes reformed, and the eyelids over them. Estin did his best to look into the regrown eyes, but couldn’t bring himself to. The body jerked once, sharply, then froze. Estin made another copy of his fighting intelligence and touched it to the head of the boy - he looked to be around fifteen, and despite Estin’s best efforts, his eyes teared up as the boy’s eyes opened and he sat upright.

Estin paused for a moment, staring at Number Two before he gave him commands. I was like you, not too long ago, Estin thought, then smiled lightly. Well, not dead, but young. Bad analogy.

He broke the gaze and had Keph give his vest to Number Two to cover him up, and then he commanded them to move on to the next grave.

With stiff fingers, Estin slowly pushed some of the dirt pack in the hole, just enough to bury the top of the now-empty coffin, and then curled back up and watched the reanimated dig - now working at two separate graves. Carefully, Estin sent Keph to grab a few more shovels and a pickaxe. Estin watched closely as Keph walked over, head down to hide the blood on his neck, to the stack of shovels and pickaxes, and commanded him to grab as many as he could. Estin perked up as he saw someone say something to Keph, and Estin closed his eyes, concentrating until he could hear through Keph’s ears.

It was an odd feeling, almost like he was distant from his own body, and without his scrying dish ready was many times harder. He focused just on Keph’s ears until he could hear through them as if they were Estin’s own, ignoring the other senses for now.

“-are you sure you’re alright? That’s a lot of blood …” someone was saying to him. Estin froze for a second, and then had Keph grunt and shake his head. This seemed to be a normal response for him, as the figure talking to him shook his or her head then shambled away from him.

Estin let himself take a deep, shuddering breath as Keph wandered back to the group, leaving the light of the lanterns. Three, now. I’m starting to build back up. I can probably get a dozen more from this side before anyone sees …

He continued periodically healing his reanimated as they worked feverishly, correcting tissue tears and rips and quickly as they occurred. Despite his best efforts to stay warm, the constant healing was keeping him colder than he would have liked to be. He surveyed the hundred-or-so graves on the un-lit side of the graveyard, and grimaced - if he didn’t find a way to stay warm, he could go into hypothermia trying to bring back this many people in the cold.

In a matter of minutes, one of the reanimated paused, and Estin approached. They had another coffin opened, this time with a more fresh body inside. Fresh to the point that the clothes were for the most part still in one piece. Estin retched a little, but the smell wasn’t as bad to him as it used to be.

On the plus side, this won’t take much heat to reanimate, Estin thought as he lowered himself into the hole. He drained the coffin lid and about half of the sides while repairing the flesh of the woman, and, with the air growing cooler around him, her body jerked. He made another copy of his fighting intelligence, put it in Number Three, and helped her out of the grave.

As the woman went to work, helping with the digging, Estin grew more anxious once more. I need to get back to Emily. I need this to go faster! Knowing Kadahn, there’s already people coming this way. I shouldn’t have taken a direct route, that’s too predictable … but I do need to get home as fast as I can.

He eyed the people who were working on the other side of the graveyard in the light. They were working at a much slower pace than the reanimated, but there were dozens of them, and they had already finished their work with nearly a quarter of the graves. I just need people to let me have one day of a head-start, Estin thought, grinding his teeth together as he huddled at the edge of the graveyard, watching his five get to work. If I could have that, and muster enough bodies, no one would get in my way, and then I wouldn’t have had to hurt Keph. He sighed, looking down at the ground. They’re only doing what they think is right. I can’t fault them for that.

His eyes turned to Keph, and he watched him dig feverishly for a moment. He cursed himself for needing to do what he had done, and thought of the man’s intelligence sphere in his backpack. I’ll bring him back later, Estin thought, trying to push away the guilt-ridden thoughts of the man’s mind being trapped in the sphere. At least the sphere was near Estin, meaning it wouldn’t fade. An hour or so outside of Estin’s presence and Keph’s intelligence sphere would fade to little more than basic reflexes.

He spent the next ten minutes moving between graves as inconspicuously as he could, reanimating more and more people. He had on of the reanimated sit with him, rubbing his hands to help keep them warm. About half of the graves had bodies too broken to be useful to him, but his reanimated worked at a feverish pace. When Number Five had been healed and brought out of his grave—an old man whose bones Estin did what little he could to strengthen—Estin had to give up his thick outer-cloak for the man to use as clothing. He was freezing now, but if anyone got sight of a naked body digging out a grave …

I needed more time to prepare, he thought, cursing himself. He looked back towards the dark village, and, although he knew it might be a mistake, gave Keph a command to go over the graveyard fence. Find clothes, Estin commanded him. And not on a person! he added as Keph grabbed at his own shirt. His fighting intelligence was rather literal and lacked basic common sense about much of anything off the battlefield.

Keph jumped over the fence and wandered back towards the cluster of buildings in the center of the town, and Estin followed up his commands by having the man scratch all the blood he could off his neck and shirt. Just in case.

Estin curled up on the ground, and had one of Numbers One and Two lay on either side of him to keep him warm. His hands were freezing—it was getting hard to move them, and healing frostbite on his own hands was probably at the limit of his abilities—but he couldn’t stop now.

A few minutes later, his hands still freezing, Estin crawled over to another recently opened grave. He examined the partially uncovered skeleton, finding it to look mostly complete and grimaced. He lowered himself inside, blew warm air on his hands, and pressed the right one against a skeleton’s forehead, the left one against the dirt mound that had been heaped up. He drained mass from the mound, and muscle and sinew began reforming around the skeleton. The eyes formed, although Estin looked away just before they did, and then the body shook. Estin once more made a copy of his fighting intelligence and pushed it into the head of the body, and it jerked upright.

Estin focused, and a few of the reanimated helped pull him out of the grave. Number Nine - an older woman, with dark hair and eyes - tried to stand, but failed. She pushed himself out of the dirt with her arms, and Estin noticed she was missing both of her feet. Estin cursed, trying to not look at the woman’s bare form, barely visible in the moonlight, and shook his chilled hands.

“What a waste of heat, she can’t even walk. Where’s Keph?”

Estin closed his eyes, and felt what Keph was feeling. He could feel something on his arms and hands, maybe coarse cloth, but Keph was too far away for Estin to focus on any more of his senses without getting a headache. What I wouldn't give for a moment to use my scrying dish right now. Let’s hope that’s enough clothing for all these, Estin thought, looking around at the shadows of his reanimated continuing to shovel. Two of his reanimated were slowly removing a corpse from a grave, careful to hold the body together as best they could. Bring it to me, Estin commanded them. He felt like his frozen arms would snap if he tried to push himself upright.

Estin huddled into a ball after giving more commands to his reanimated. He waited barely a minute before he felt one of them drawing closer. It’s Keph! Estin realized, noticing his signature. But what’s that noise?

Estin got on his knees and peeked through the wrought-iron fence around the graveyard. There’s Keph … but who’s that next to him?

Estin closed his eyes and focused hard. He clenches his hands in anticipation; without a scrying dish this would hurt. He felt as though his head were being gouged in half with an ax, and then he connected fully to Keph’s mind, seeing through his eyes, hearing through his ears, feeling what he touched He felt rough cloth on his arms, and then Keph’s vision snapped into focus.

“- are you doing?” Estin heard the figure next to Keph say. “Why won’t you talk to me?”

Estin turned Keph’s head and saw a younger woman hurrying to keep up with Keph. As Keph looked at her, Estin felt something, a trace of a memory from Keph’s mind. Oh, by the night … this is his wife.

“Slivers,” Estin cursed, trying to stand while maintaining his tight hold on Keph’s mind. I knew this was dumb, but what choice did I have? Oh no … what do I do?

He opened Keph’s mouth, about to say something reassuring, but just as he did, Estin heard a voice through his own ears. It sounded far away, as if someone were speaking on the other side of a wall, but it caught Estin’s attention instantly.

“Ho down there, need some light?”

“No, no, we’re good!” Estin yelled back at a quickly approaching figure who was holding a lamp. His head throbbed at his split focus, and then he lost control of Keph. Not now, please don’t let everything fall apart now.

“We’re fine, we like the dark,” Estin cried out, his voice cracking. Idiot! Why would I say that?

The man walked closer, twisting the key on the lamp to bring more light and lifting it to illuminate the area just as Estin turned back to the approaching Keph. The region was lit up, and Estin saw the two naked reanimated carrying a corpse towards him, just as he had commanded. He saw Keph’s wife freeze, staring over the fence at the morbid scene, and he watched, grimacing, as she opened her mouth.

Things seemed to slow down as Estin sent out rapid mental commands to each of his reanimated, and his focus broke as she began to scream.

In the next moment Keph clapped his hand over her mouth, but the damage was done. Estin turned to see the portly figure carrying the lamp take a step closer and freeze.

“Diana?” the man asked, staring in shock at one of the reanimated.

“Go, go, go!” Estin screamed, giving a verbal command, and the reanimated attacked, a few tackling the man and putting him in a chokehold while others charged towards him, holding their shovels and pickaxes. Estin heard screams, and, examining the graveyard, saw people pointing his direction.

“The Necrite’s here!” Estin heard someone yell, and he grimaced as he charged after his reanimated. He paused briefly to warm his freezing fingers near the shattered lamp which had spilled its burning oil all over the ground, and then had his other few reanimated circle around him, leaving the man unconscious and Keph’s wife sobbing on the ground.

He looked across the graveyard and saw dozens of workers approaching, most were holding picks or shovels, but they were crowded together, too afraid to advance towards him quickly. About half of the people hung back, confused and frightened.

Estin had his reanimated part, making him more visible, and called towards the approaching workers. “We’re leaving! I wish you no harm, I just need to leave.”

“Keph!” Estin heard Keph’s wife yell from behind him, and he grimaced. “Keph, what are you doing?”

“That’s not Keph!” Estin heard one of the workers yell, and more voices chimed in.

“He killed him!”

”He used his witchcraft on him!”

“Don’t hurt them,” Estin commanded his reanimated as he knelt down, Keph and Number One’s body joining him. “Hold them away for a few moments.”

He placed his hands on Number One’s chest, questing out into the man’s form until he had a mental image of it, and then focused, strengthening every muscle simultaneously. The body swelled, quickly becoming grossly muscled. The man, who had been thin before, now had biceps thicker than Estin’s thighs. As soon as Estin pulled his hands away Number One stood; still the same height, still the same basic shape, but stronger than any regular human could be.

“Defend us, but hurt no one,” Estin commanded. “Then be at peace.”

Number One sprinted out of the circle, tackling the nearest villager. Estin turned back to Keph, ignoring the noises of shovel on pickaxe from just paces away.

He focused, pulling the copy of his fighting intelligence out of Keph’s head. He dismissed it and it turned to dust while he reached into his bag. He placed his hand on Keph’s neck, questing through his body, ignoring the screams and noise around him. Five reanimated with his fighting intelligence could hold off more than triple their number in untrained villagers, but with the command to not hurt anyone, they’d be less effective. He felt a growth near the man’s kidney, and purged it out of his body. After strengthening a few other muscles, and ligaments he pulled out Keph’s intelligence—his mind, his soul—and pushed it back into Keph’s body.

Keph’s eyes snapped open, and he took in a deep, ragged breath. “You’re fine,” Estin said to the man as he blinked bleary eyes. “Healthier than you were a half hour ago, in fact. And again, I’m sorry.”

Estin stood, knowing from experience that Keph would take a moment to begin moving. Even if he did recover quickly, Estin had a link with him now; it was harder to control conscious people he’d brought back, but once he’d reanimated someone, the link would only fade with distance, never with time.

Estin cursed himself again, his mind flashing back to what had happened only a few days before. He remembered Kadahn’s reaction, and felt a familiar sensation in the back of his head, like a rhythmic, soothing melody trying to lull him into memory …

He clenched his fists and forced himself back into the moment even as memories threatened to overwhelm him. I can’t pass out, not now. Estin glanced around, letting the noise wash over him.

Only a handful of the villagers were actually engaging the reanimated around Estin, about five of them were swinging shovels or pickaxes, all strikes deftly blocked by his embodied copies of his fighting intelligence, but more were joining in. He saw people running from outside the graveyard, a few of them bearing swords, and noticed Number One grabbing onto a man’s leg, still obeying Estin even as a half dozen people broke his body with their tools. Estin felt a wave of loss for the sacrifice, but reminded himself that his fighting intelligence couldn’t feel—it was a mind, but not a conscience.

“Let’s go!” Estin commanded, and his four remaining reanimated circled him once more, running in perfect sync with his step. The one in front vaulted over the stone wall, and Estin leapt over, the other three reanimated right behind him, ready to catch him if he even so much as worried about losing balance. They headed towards the other end of the village, heading roughly south, the moonlight bright enough to illuminate the cobbled road they were running on. One of the reanimated veered off from the pack, grabbing the clothes that Keph had brought, and Estin slowed down to wait for him until an arrow sprouted from Number Three’s back. He quested out and felt Kay gliding along overhead, following their group.

They charged out of the town, following the main road south for a few minutes before he felt he had enough of a lead to pause. Not that I think any of them are following me, he thought, leaning forward and taking gulping breaths. As the reanimated distributed the clothes, Kay descended through the trees, landing on Number Two’s head.

She croaked, ruffled her feathers, and flapped her wings anxiously. Estin held out his arm and Kay jumped onto it as Number Two pulled a shirt on. “That went better than I feared,” Estin muttered as Kay shimmied towards his shoulder. Estin sensed a thought through their bond, even as she tried to repress it. “Yes, yes, I know. You were right to be scared of them.”

“I wasn’t going to say that,” Kay croaked, her voice raspy in her raven’s body.

Estin nodded, taking a few deep breaths and exerting his power to help rejuvenate his tired muscles. There was only so much he could do on any living body, let alone his own, but with enough focus he could run for much longer than most people.

Number Two pulled the arrow out of Number Three’s back, and Estin leaned forward to heal the body. When the would faded, not even leaving a scar, the reanimated finished dressing and Estin nodded to the body, trying to be nonchalant.

“You want that one, Kay?”

Kay ruffled her feathers, not meeting Estin’s gaze. Estin felt a hint of what she was thinking, but tried to ignore those thoughts. “I—I think being able to fly will be more useful.”

Estin smiled softly. “Maybe tomorrow.”

“Maybe tomorrow,” Kay repeated in her croaking voice. “Flying will be more useful,” she repeated, then cawed a laugh. “Gotta watch after my little brother.”

He nodded and pulled his compass out of his bag while his reanimated stood in a defensive position around him, each still holding a pickaxe or shovel.

We’re going … south-southeast. We need to go almost directly south for a few hours at this pace, and then we can cut into the woods. There’s too many villages on this road, and knowing Kadahn, there’s dozens of riders coming after me. I need to keep moving. And if the king sent anyone … I need to get to Emily before it’s too late.

It might already be.
